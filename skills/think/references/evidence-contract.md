# The Evidence Contract

Every skill in the Think-Break-Build system follows this contract to ensure reviews are grounded in verifiable reality.

## Evidence Hierarchy
Prefer sources approximately in this order:
1. governing laws, regulations, contractual requirements, policies, and authoritative organizational records;
2. official standards and specifications;
3. first-party documentation;
4. peer-reviewed research and systematic reviews;
5. established professional frameworks from recognized institutions;
6. credible secondary analysis;
7. unsupported common practice.

Secondary sources may help discover stronger sources. Do not turn something into a "best practice" simply because several websites repeat it.

## Claim Classification
Material claims should be distinguishable as:
* **Verified** — supported directly by evidence.
* **Supported** — good evidence exists but requires contextual interpretation.
* **Inferred** — reasoned conclusion derived from available evidence.
* **Assumed** — required for the analysis but not yet established.
* **Unknown** — insufficient information.

Never present an inference as an established fact.

## Source Fit
Before applying an external framework, ask:
> "Does this source govern or meaningfully apply to this artifact and context?"

Do not mechanically apply software, risk, project-management, change-management, or governance frameworks merely because they are familiar. Select frameworks according to the artifact.

## Citation Rule
Any recommendation presented as being grounded in an external standard, research finding, regulatory requirement, or recognized framework must identify its source.

Recommendations derived primarily through reasoning should say so.
