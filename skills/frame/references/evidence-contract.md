# The Evidence Contract

Every skill in the Frame → Break → Fortify system follows this contract so analysis stays grounded in evidence rather than unsupported common practice.

## Evidence Hierarchy

Prefer sources approximately in this order:
1. governing laws, regulations, contractual requirements, mandatory policies, and authoritative organizational records;
2. official standards and specifications;
3. authoritative first-party documentation and institutional frameworks;
4. peer-reviewed research and systematic reviews;
5. established professional frameworks from recognized institutions;
6. credible secondary analysis;
7. unsupported common practice.

Secondary sources may help discover stronger sources. Repetition across websites does not turn a claim into a best practice.

## Claim Classification

Material claims should be distinguishable as:
* **Verified** — directly supported by evidence.
* **Supported** — good evidence exists but requires contextual interpretation.
* **Inferred** — reasoned conclusion derived from available evidence.
* **Assumed** — required for analysis but not yet established.
* **Unknown** — insufficient information.

Never present an inference as an established fact.

## Source Fit

Before applying a framework, standard, study, or methodology, ask whether it meaningfully applies to the artifact, domain, jurisdiction, population, and decision being made.

Do not mechanically apply a familiar framework merely because it is well known.

## Source Quality vs Selection

Source quality is determined by authority, evidence strength, and applicability. User approval determines whether a source is selected for use. Approval does not upgrade a weak source into reliable evidence.

For Fortify, AI-found material design-basis sources remain candidates until the user approves their use. Material recommendations should rely on applicable governing sources or reliable approved sources. Supporting evidence may corroborate them but should not be misrepresented as stronger than it is.

If reliable search or retrieval is unavailable, do not fabricate sources or pretend they were verified.

## Citation Rule

Recommendations grounded in an external standard, research finding, regulatory requirement, framework, or recognized methodology must identify the source.

Recommendations derived primarily through reasoning should say so and must not be presented as established best practice.
